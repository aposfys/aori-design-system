The drawn register

One hand, three jobs now. The paintings carry the romance and the icons do the interface work; between them sat a gap — the quiet, service moments where a painting is too loud and a 24px icon is too small. An empty bag. A search that found nothing. A care instruction. The drawn register fills that gap: the functional set's own 2px carbon line, grown to a 96px grid, with one pigment accent per drawing and nothing else. These are bench drawings, not paintings — made here, in the icons' hand, and they claim no provenance from the painted plates.

Rules

Where a drawing goes, and where it never does

  • Service, not romanceDrawings take the service surfaces: empty states, care and sizing instructions, transactional email, booking confirmations. A drawing never opens a page, never sells a piece, and never sits on the same surface as a painted plate — beside a painting, the painting wins and the drawing goes.
  • Construction96px grid, the system's 2px carbon rule (#2A2622), butt caps, mitre joins, square corners. Rendered at 96px, at 64px minimum, at 160px maximum. Never recoloured, never given a background of its own.
  • One accentEach drawing carries exactly one pigment — aegean, terracotta, saffron or olive — on the element that carries the meaning. Two accents make it a picture, and pictures are the paintings' job.
  • Words beside itLike an icon, a drawing never carries meaning alone: the annotation line under it says the thing plainly. "All twelve gone." "Keep above the water."
  • The motif law holdsA drawing never redraws a painted motif — no drawn octopus, no drawn sun, no drawn spiral. Motifs stay paintings; drawings depict objects from the bench and the shop, nothing with a place's name on it.
