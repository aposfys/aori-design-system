AORI · Iconography · The functional set

Sixteen marks,
one bench

The motifs are paintings and stay paintings. The interface needs a different tool: a functional set that can say search, basket, account, close — jobs no painting should be asked to do. The two sets never trade jobs: a motif never acts as a control, and an icon never decorates.

01

Construction

Every icon is drawn once, on a 24px grid with a 16px keystone — the live area the stroke may not leave except for a deliberate overshoot on diagonals. The stroke is 2px, butt caps, mitre joins: the same carbon rule that draws the system's borders, so an icon sits in a layout like a rule that learned a shape. Corners are square because every corner in the system is square. Circles are lawful — motifs are circles, and the law holds here too.

The terracotta frame is the 16px keystone inside the 24px grid. Fills are forbidden with one exception: the 1.6px centre dot of seal, which marks the hallmark and is the set's one solid.

02

The set

Sixteen icons. A new icon needs a job no existing icon covers, drawn on the same grid, at the same stroke — or it does not enter the set.

  • currentColor, alwaysAn icon takes the colour of the text it sits with — carbon ink on paper, paper on pigment. It is never given a colour of its own, and never a pigment field of its own.
  • Two rendered sizes24px beside body text and in nav; 16px inside the clerical stone register and dense tables. Above 40px an icon becomes decoration, and decoration is the motifs' job.
  • Icon plus wordAn icon never carries meaning alone. Buttons and nav say the word; the icon confirms it. The exceptions are close and the arrows, which carry aria-label.
