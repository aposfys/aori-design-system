AORI · Photography · Resolved

Image jobs

Four treatments and nine positions failed because they were all answers to the wrong question. I was trying to make a photograph look like a painting. The question that matters is what each picture is for.

01

The finding

Every commercial art-direction source describes the same structure, and none of them describes blending. Product imagery is tiered by job: hero visuals express the emotion of the collection, the reason behind the piece — story and tone, not detail; e-commerce images are where light, distance and composition stay identical across collections, so a customer can trust what they are looking at. The conventional sequence is a clean hero, then context, then a tight detail shot, and the brands that perform best are the ones that assign those jobs clearly rather than asking one image to do everything.

Two consequences for AORI. First, an illustration-anchored hero is a normal pattern, not a workaround — a custom illustration serving as the visual anchor in place of photography is an established option, not a compromise made because the photographs are wrong. Second, the tier that kept failing in my treatments is the middle one — the lifestyle image that tries to be both emotional and evidential at once. AORI does not need that tier. The paintings already do the emotional work better than any photograph of a hand would.

The correction

Painting and photography were never supposed to look like each other. They are supposed to appear in different places, doing different jobs, and never compete for the same slot. There is no blend, no filter, no shared grain. There is a division of labour.

02

Two tiers, and who owns each

Most brands run three tiers. AORI runs two, because the paintings absorb the middle one. This is the whole rule.

Tier one · Painting
Why the piece exists

Tone, place, story, season, the reason a collection is called what it is. Never asked to show a clasp or a size. A painting is allowed to be inaccurate about the object because it is not describing the object.

Owns

Home hero. Collection headers. Story and journal. Packaging. Brand book. Instagram place posts. Every section ground.

Tier two · Photograph
What the piece is

Material, scale, finish, hallmark, how it sits on a body. Identical light and distance across every collection, forever, so a customer can compare two pieces and trust the comparison. Never restyled to look painted.

Owns

Product gallery. Size and fit. The hallmark detail. Stockist and press files. Instagram product posts.

The rule that replaces all nine positions

A painting and a photograph never occupy the same slot, and neither is ever restyled toward the other. Where they appear on one page, a horizontal ink rule and a Commissioner label separate them, exactly as two sections of a form are separated. The seam is declared, not hidden — which is the same logic the receipt-sheet grounds and the ledger fields already run on.

03

The shotlist

Tier two needs no art direction, only consistency — which makes it cheap. One session per run produces every photograph the system needs; the trade guidance is to shoot the variants in the same session and crop per placement rather than reshooting, so this is four frames per piece and nothing more.

The specification, so every run matches
LightOne window, daylight, no flash, no reflector. Shadow soft and to one side. GroundSkin, plain cotton, or unpainted lime plaster. Never wood, never linen weave, never dark cloth. ColourShot and left warm. No cooling, no filter, no crushed blacks. Silver reads grey, not blue. CropSquare for the gallery, 4:5 for Instagram, both cropped from one frame. NeverProps. Flowers. Two pieces from different collections in one frame. A painting in the background.

The seam, shown

06

What this unblocks

  • Nothing is waiting on a decision any more. The two failed exploration pages are retired; this replaces them.
  • The storefront UI kit can be built now — paintings on the hero and collection headers, photographs in the grid and gallery, ink rule at the seam.
  • No reshoot is needed to start. The nine stand-ins already fit tier two's specification. When AORI's own four-frame sets arrive they drop into the same slots.
  • The middle tier stays deliberately empty. No styled lifestyle photography, ever. That is the tier that was making everything look wrong.
