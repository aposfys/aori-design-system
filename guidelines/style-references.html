Style references

This design system is built on a style, not on one person. The style has a name in the trade — flat opaque gouache with naive figuration and hand-lettered titles — and a clear lineage. What follows is where it comes from, the eight traits that define it, and where to look. No third-party images are reproduced here; the demonstrations are plain colour fields drawn in CSS.

Lineage

Where the vocabulary comes from
1943 – 1954
Matisse cut-outs

Sheets hand-painted in gouache, then cut freehand and pinned. Drawing with scissors. Establishes the whole grammar: unmodulated colour, organic silhouettes, imprecise edges as a virtue.

1890s – 1910s
Naive painting

Rousseau and the self-taught tradition. Flattened perspective, deliberate awkwardness in the figure, dreamlike calm. This is where the charmingly clumsy figure comes from.

1940s – 1960s
Commercial gouache

Gouache was the default for posters, fashion plates and animation because it reproduced flat and bright. This is why the style reads as printed, not as painted.

2015 – now
Small-press and riso

Zines, artist books and risograph printing. Limited ink sets, visible registration, hand-lettered covers, short runs. Supplies the paper stock and the informality.

The eight traits

What makes something read as this style
Unmodulated colour

Every shape is one flat colour, laid opaque, edge to edge. No shading, no gradient, no tint of the same hue. A lighter red is pink — a different pigment, not the same pigment weakened.

The imprecise edge

Shapes stop where the brush or the scissors stopped. Edges wobble, corners are not square, the field does not reach the paper margin evenly. Perfection breaks the illusion faster than anything else.

Paper as the ground, not the background

The unpainted paper is an active part of the composition — usually 30–40% of the page. It is warm off-white, never pure white, and never a texture applied on top of a colour.

A small closed palette

Four to six pigments per piece, drawn from the same tube set. The saturation is high but the hues are earthy — chalk in them, never neon. One connective colour links the rest.

Flattened space

No perspective, no horizon line, no cast shadow. Objects sit on the picture plane rather than in a room. Scale is decided by importance, not by distance.

Water
Hand-lettered titles, tracked wide

Titles are drawn, not set. They are uppercase because printing by hand favours caps, and they are spaced generously because the hand leaves gaps a font would not. Wide tracking is the cheapest way to fake this.

The circle as the only curve

Everything else is a straight-sided field. The full circle — a dot of paint, a sun, a fruit — is the single geometric curve the style allows. It becomes the bullet, the marker and the ornament.

isn’t about
the outcome
Nothing is corrected

A struck-through word stays struck through. A missing apostrophe stays missing. Registration is off by a millimetre. These are not errors to fix — they are the evidence that a person made it, which is the entire commercial argument for the style in 2026.

What is ours and what is not

Every image in assets/ is cropped from the six photographs supplied with this brief — nothing has been taken from any reference above. The palette in tokens/colors.css was sampled from those same photographs. The references exist to argue about direction and to prove the style has a history; they are not a source of assets.

If this system is going to belong to a brand rather than to the artist who painted the source, it needs its own artwork commissioned in this vocabulary. The eight traits above are the brief you would hand an illustrator.
