01 — The brand

In one page

AORI makes jewellery drawn from Crete. Not souvenirs of the island — pieces that take their names, their colours and their forms from specific places on it. Made by hand, in runs small enough to count.

Every piece is named for a place — a ridge, a stretch of water, a terrace of olive trees, a room in a palace four thousand years old. The name is not decoration. It tells you which colours the piece uses, what shape it takes, and why it exists. A piece without a place is not an AORI piece.

The brand looks like the island rather than like a jeweller. Lime-plaster grounds, pigment laid flat and opaque, square corners, painted plates with the paper showing at their margins. There are no gradients anywhere in this system, no drop shadows on controls, and no icon library. What a normal brand would say with a gloss, this one says with a colour.

Made by hand

Cast, filed, hammered, soldered, strung. Named verbs, never "crafted with love".

Named for a place

Psiloritis, Livyko, Elia, Knosos. The place sets the palette.

Counted, not endless

Runs of ten or twelve. When they are gone we make more, but not the same.

Worn, not displayed

On hands that cook, swim and work. Wear is the test, not the showcase.

A piece is finished when it can be worn every day without being thought about.

01 — The brand

The name

Four letters, three syllables — a·o·ri — with the weight on the middle. Plain Latin letters, no accent and no ornament: the name is short enough that it does not need help. It is what lengthens the O and stops the word being said as two beats.

Written in capitals it is simply AORI. Latin only, everywhere. The account writes it as AORI Handmade Jewelry, and that is the full descriptive form. Never abbreviated, possessive or pluralised. In running prose it takes no article: "AORI makes jewellery drawn from Crete", not "the AORI". Where a legal name is required, the full form is AORI Jewellery.

How many, and in what proportion

Four to six pigments carry a composition; never more. The ground takes most of the area, one pigment leads, two or three support, and one small thing is allowed to shout. Get the proportion wrong and the palette stops looking like Crete and starts looking like a chart.

The proportion, as a bar
GroundLeadSupportSupportShout
Rules that follow from it
  • Two pigments of the same temperature never share a large field. Aegean and shallow, yes; terracotta and rust, no.
  • Saffron is a highlight. If it exceeds about a tenth of the area, it reads as a warning sign.
  • Cypress and umber are the two colours allowed to hold down a whole page — nothing else goes full-bleed dark.
  • A pigment placed on a pigment needs a lime or stock gap between them. Painted plates already carry theirs.

Tracking, case, and the rules

Wide tracking on capitals is the loudest signal in this brand. It is also the easiest thing to get wrong: tracked caps at body size are unreadable, and untracked caps at display size look like a mistake.

Four tracking values, and where each belongs
Livyko
0.14em · display

One word, big. Posters, covers, hero titles.

Livyko
0.09em · title

Titles of more than one word, section headers.

Oxidised bands
0.055em · annotation

Every set of capitals in the sans. Labels, buttons, nav.

Oxidised silver bands
0.01em · prose

Running text. Effectively normal — do not track prose.

Do
  • Set names and titles in caps, in the display face, tracked wide.
  • Set prose in sentence case, in the serif, untracked.
  • Set AORI in the display face, tracked wide, never in the sans.
  • Let a long title wrap rather than shrinking it below its floor.
  • Use one weight of Didot and let size carry the hierarchy.
Do not
  • Track a paragraph. Wide tracking belongs to capitals only.
  • Set a sentence of prose in the display face.
  • Fake a bold or an italic Didot — neither exists here.
  • Use all caps for anything longer than about six words.
  • Centre a paragraph. Titles may centre; prose is flush left.

05 — Image

The hand

One hand, two jobs. Everything is painted in watercolour and gouache on cotton paper — saturated fields held inside a drawn contour, the paper showing through the lighter passages. The motifs are not a second style: each one is a detail lifted out of a finished painting and cropped tight, which is why the whole system reads as one body of work.

The full platelandscape · figures · still life
  • Colour sits in saturated fields, each one held inside a drawn contour.
  • The paper reads through the lighter passages — nothing is opaque white.
  • Chroma is high and unmodulated. Nothing is greyed to make it recede.
  • Mounted with a 5px unpainted margin on toned stock, so it never floats on the page.
The lifted detailmotifs · marks · small sizes
  • Cut from a painting, never drawn separately. The brush marks match because they are the same brush marks.
  • Cropped square from the plate, then knocked out or discked only where a mark is needed.
  • Reads at 40px because the contour is already there in the source.
  • A mark is never invented. If a subject has no painting, it has no mark.

Always and never

Always
  • Mount a plate on toned stock with an unpainted margin around it.
  • Keep the paper tooth visible. Flatten it and the whole thing looks rendered.
  • Let the jewellery be small and matter-of-fact inside the picture — a hoop, a band, a stone.
  • Square corners. The circle is the only curve in the system.
  • One hand per image: pastel or ink, never both.
Never
  • Gradients, cast shadows, perspective tricks, ambient occlusion.
  • Metallic shine on silver — a flat limestone field is the silver.
  • A drop shadow behind a plate to lift it off the page.
  • Stock photography, clip art, emoji, or anything that reads as software.
  • Rounding the corners of an image, or masking it into a circle.
✓Mounted on stock, margin intact, square corners.
✕Floating on white with a shadow under it.
✕Masked into a circle. Plates are rectangles.
✕Pushed saturation. The palette is already the palette.
On photography

There is none yet, and the illustrations stand in for it everywhere. When it arrives the standard is daylight, real surfaces, and jewellery on hands that use them — not white seamless, not velvet, not a light tent. No teal-and-orange grading, and no shallow focus that erases the place.

Photography

Photography carries the product; illustration carries the place. Two frames, not interchangeable. The catalogue frame is the piece alone on a seamless pigment ground, one colour per collection. The worn frame is the piece on a body, in daylight, close, and gives the scale the catalogue frame cannot. Every product needs one of each.

Frame 01 — the catalogue frame

The piece alone, centred, filling about half the frame height, square. The ground is a flat palette pigment and it changes with the collection, so a tile announces its run before the name is read. Soft even light, one direction, no reflector. Matte silver reads neutral grey and the file marks stay visible — that texture is the product.

Colour

Focus

Never

Props, flowers, jewellery boxes, coffee cups, marble tiles, mirrors, water droplets, a second piece competing for the eye. In the catalogue frame nothing enters but silver and the pigment; in the worn frame nothing but skin, cloth and silver.

Motifs

Six marks, every one of them cut out of a finished painting. They do the job an icon set would do in another brand — a section opener, a divider, a stamp on a pouch, a highlight cover — which is why this system needs no icon library. Each belongs to a place, so using one is a claim about which place a page is about. Four are knocked out of their painted field; two are cropped to a disc, which is why those two read as seals rather than marks.

How to use one
  • One motif per page or per post. Two is a pattern, and patterns are on the next page.
  • Minimum 40px. Below that the wash muddies — use nothing instead.
  • Never redraw one. A mark that does not exist in a painting does not exist.
  • On lime, on stock, or on a pigment field — the knocked-out marks hold on all three. A disc mark carries its own ground, so it needs no help.
  • Not as a bullet, a checkbox, or a UI icon. These are marks, not glyphs.

Motion

Things fade and settle. Nothing bounces, springs, pops or slides in from off-screen. The house easing is a soft settle — quick to start, slow to stop — and the longest thing in the system takes 700ms.

Durations
120msA colour change on hover or press. 220msA control changing state; a hairline appearing. 320msA panel or menu opening. 480msA plate fading in as it enters the viewport. 700msA page transition. The ceiling — nothing is slower.
Easing
Settlecubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1) — the default for everything. Entercubic-bezier(0,0,.35,1) — something arriving. Exitcubic-bezier(.4,0,1,1) — something leaving, faster. LinearProgress bars only. Never on a visible object.
What moves, and how
HoverColour only. A button darkens; a link changes hue; a plate does nothing at all. PressColour only. Nothing shrinks, lifts, or scales. RevealOpacity 0 → 1 over 480ms, with at most a 12px rise. No slide from the side. MenuFade the panel in over 320ms. The trigger does not rotate or morph. GalleryCrossfade between plates. No horizontal slide, no parallax, no zoom.
Never
  • Springs, bounces, overshoot, elastic easing.

  • Counters that tick up, text that types itself in, letters that stagger.

07 — Words

How we write

Plain, unhurried, specific about place. Say where it comes from and how it was made, then stop. The voice is someone who makes things, telling you exactly what this is — not a brand telling you how to feel about it.

Name the place

Every piece has one. A piece without a place is not an AORI piece, and a description without the place is unfinished.

Give the number

Metres, millimetres, grams, run size, days. "2,456 metres" earns more trust than "towering".

Verbs, not adjectives

Cast, filed, hammered, soldered, strung, polished. Never "crafted with love", never "artisanal".

Admit the limit

"No rush orders in August." Scarcity is stated as a fact and never used as pressure — no countdowns, no "only 2 left!".

Sentence case

Prose and headings in sentence case. Capitals belong to names, labels and buttons — not to sentences.

We, and you

"We make", "we ship". The reader is "you". No third person, and no "the customer".

Greek where it belongs

Place names are written in Latin transliteration: Psiloritis, Livyko, Elia, Knosos. Greek script is not used anywhere.

180,00 €

Comma decimal, space, symbol after. No exclamation marks, no emoji, no ALL-CAPS shouting.

Short, then stop

A product description is three or four sentences. If it needs more, the piece needs a story page, not a longer caption.

Motif does the work here
Print cannot carry a painted plate cheaply — halftones on kraft board go muddy. Motifs solve it: single-colour, hard-edged, and they survive a screen print, a rubber stamp and a 32 mm sticker. One motif per surface, chosen for the collection inside.
Ink count
Two inks maximum per surface, and terracotta is one of them on anything a customer opens. The seals are the exception: four one-colour variants printed as a set, so a single order covers a year.
Nothing is precious
No ribbon, no magnetic closure, no tissue folded into a fan. The box is a box. What makes it feel made is the pouch, the handwriting on the tag, and the fact that the care slip admits the silver will change.

09 — Rules

Keep and avoid

If you read one page of this book, read this one. Everything above is the reasoning; these two lists are the outcome.

Keep
  • Lime plaster as the ground. Nothing sits on white.
  • Large margins. The work needs air, and so does the page.
  • Flat, opaque colour in whole fields. Four to six per composition.
  • Square corners on every surface and control.
  • Didot caps tracked wide for names; the serif, untracked, for prose.
  • AORI in Didot caps, tracked wide, wherever the name appears.
  • Painted plates on toned stock, with the unpainted margin intact.
  • One terracotta action per screen; aegean, underlined, for links.
  • Fades and settles, 120–700ms. Colour-only hover and press.
  • Prices as 180,00 €, and a run size stated as a number.
Avoid
  • Gradients of any kind, including metallic shine on silver.
  • Drop shadows on cards, buttons and menus.
  • Rounded rectangles, pills, chips. The circle is the only curve.
  • Icon libraries. Seven painted motifs exist; that is the set.
  • Frosted glass, blur, translucency, anything that reads as chrome.
  • Luxury vocabulary: curated, timeless, elevated, bespoke.
  • Stock photography, and teal-and-orange grading.
  • Springs, bounces, parallax, counters, typewriter text.
  • Two patterns on one screen, or a pattern under a headline.
  • Emoji, exclamation marks, countdowns, "only 2 left".
